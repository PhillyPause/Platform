**Stop training AI systems more capable than the current frontier, until we can
show they are safe.** Not forever. Not all AI. The largest training runs,
paused, until the safety case exists.

That is the whole proposal. Everything below is detail about how it would work.

## The specific asks

1. **A moratorium on frontier training runs** above a compute threshold,
   binding on companies rather than voluntary.
2. **A licensing regime** for training above that threshold: you demonstrate
   safety first, then you train — the arrangement that already governs
   aviation, pharmaceuticals and nuclear power.
3. **Tracking of the hardware.** Frontier training needs enormous quantities
   of specialised chips from a very small number of suppliers. That is the
   enforcement lever.
4. **An international body** with real authority, because a national-only
   pause moves the work rather than stopping it.
5. **Liability** that lands on the developer, so the cost of a harm sits with
   whoever chose to risk it.

## Why compute is the lever

A pause sounds unenforceable until you look at the supply chain. Training a
frontier model requires tens of thousands of specialised accelerators, built
by a handful of firms, on machines from essentially one company in the
Netherlands. Chips are physical, countable, and already export-controlled.

This is the detail that moves sceptics: we are not proposing to police ideas or
mathematics. We are proposing to count hardware, which governments demonstrably
already know how to do.

## Four levels of regulation

Useful to keep separate, because people argue past each other by conflating
them:

| Level | What it covers | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Transparency** | Report what you are training, disclose incidents | Partly in place |
| **2. Evaluation** | Mandatory dangerous-capability testing before release | Drafted in places, weakly enforced |
| **3. Licensing** | Permission required before a frontier run | Our ask |
| **4. Moratorium** | Hard halt above a threshold until safety is shown | Our ask |

Levels 1 and 2 are good and insufficient: a report filed about a system nobody
can interpret, and a test designed for capabilities we already know to look
for, do not constitute control.

## Objections

The honest ones deserve answers, not slogans — China, innovation,
enforceability, the risk that a pause helps the wrong people. They have
[their own page](/ask/objections).

## What it would cost

Less than people assume. A pause above a frontier compute threshold leaves
essentially every current beneficial application untouched: medical imaging,
protein folding, translation, accessibility tools, logistics. These do not run
at the frontier and would not be affected.

What would be delayed is the race to build something that can replace human
cognition wholesale. Delay in that specific project is not a cost. It is the
point.
