Everything on this page is specific to here. It is the part a national
organisation cannot do for us.

## Why Philadelphia matters for this

Not sentiment — arithmetic. Pennsylvania sends **two senators and seventeen
representatives** to Washington, and it is a state where offices are
genuinely responsive to constituent contact because they expect to be
contested.

There is also a concentration of relevant institutions in a walkable
radius: Penn, Drexel and Temple, a large hospital and biotech sector that uses
AI daily, and a civic culture that is unusually comfortable with the idea that
a few determined people in a room can change something.

## Where we table

Rotating, weather permitting:

- **University City** — around Penn and Drexel during term. The best
  conversations, and the most pushback, which is useful.
- **Clark Park farmers' market**, Saturdays. Mixed crowd, very willing to
  talk.
- **Temple's main campus**, Broad Street side.
- **Rittenhouse and Center City** for weekday lunch crowds.

Tabling needs no preparation beyond showing up. We bring the table, the
flyers and a one-page script. [Come along](/join).

## Campuses

Students and faculty are the fastest route to a local press story and to more
volunteers. What we are looking for:

- A **faculty contact** in CS, philosophy, law or public policy willing to
  host a talk.
- **Student groups** — AI safety, effective altruism, ethics, policy debate —
  to co-host.
- Anyone who can book a room. This is a bigger bottleneck than it sounds.

If you teach or study at Penn, Drexel, Temple, Villanova, Swarthmore, Haverford
or Bryn Mawr, [get in touch](/about).

## Harrisburg

State legislatures cannot regulate frontier training runs — that is federal
and international. But they are not irrelevant:

- **Procurement.** What the Commonwealth will and will not buy, and what
  safety terms it demands from vendors.
- **State employment and services.** Rules on automated decision-making in
  hiring, benefits and policing.
- **Resolutions.** Non-binding, but a state resolution urging federal action
  is a real signal and generates press.

This is early for us and we would like help from anyone who has worked in
state politics.

## Local press

Outlets that matter here, roughly in order of how much a story in them moves
things:

- **The Philadelphia Inquirer** — letters to the editor get read by
  congressional staff.
- **WHYY** and **Billy Penn** — receptive to local-angle civic stories.
- **Campus papers** — *The Daily Pennsylvanian*, *The Triangle*, *The Temple
  News*. Low barrier, and they get picked up.
- **Neighbourhood newsletters and civic associations** — small audiences,
  high trust.

The angle that works is always local: Philadelphians organising, not a
national issue explained.

## The regional picture

We are independent, so there is no national body handing us a playbook. In
practice that means we borrow what works from other groups and publish what we
learn — our materials are open-licensed precisely so anyone organising in
another city can take them.

Pittsburgh is the obvious partner for a statewide push, given Carnegie Mellon's
position in AI research. If you are organising there, or anywhere else in
Pennsylvania, [get in touch](/about) — we would rather coordinate than
duplicate.
