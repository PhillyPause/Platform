Congressional offices tally constituent contacts by issue. They are not
counting arguments; they are counting people. This is why a phone call from a
constituent outranks a very good essay from someone out of state.

## Who to contact

**Both of Pennsylvania's US senators**, and **the one representative for your
district**. Only your own district's representative counts — offices discard
contacts from outside.

Find your representative by ZIP code at
[house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative](https://www.house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative),
and your senators at [senate.gov](https://www.senate.gov/senators/senators-contact.htm).

We are assembling a Pennsylvania-specific directory — who sits on which
relevant committee, what each office has said about AI, and which staffer
handles technology. Until that lands, the links above are the reliable route.

## What to ask for

Pick **one** ask. A call that requests one specific thing is worth more than a
call that surveys five.

- **Support binding limits on frontier AI training**, not voluntary
  commitments.
- **Support mandatory pre-deployment safety evaluation** by an independent
  body, not by the developer.
- **Support liability for AI developers** for harms their systems cause.
- **Ask the office to state a position** on AI extinction risk. An office with
  no position is the most movable kind.

## The script

Adapt it; do not read it word for word. Thirty seconds is fine.

> Hello, my name is [name] and I am a constituent in [neighbourhood], ZIP
> [code].
>
> I am calling because I am worried about the race to build AI systems more
> capable than humans. The CEOs of the companies building them have signed a
> statement saying it could cause human extinction, and there is still no
> binding limit on how large these systems can get.
>
> I would like [Senator / Representative name] to support mandatory safety
> requirements on frontier AI training, rather than leaving it to the
> companies.
>
> Can you tell me whether the office has a position on this?

Then stop talking. If they ask questions, answer honestly — including "I do not
know, but I can send you something." Offices respect that more than bluffing.

## What actually happens

A staffer or an intern answers, takes your ZIP code and your issue, and logs
it. It takes under two minutes and is almost always polite. You will probably
get a form letter weeks later.

That is not failure. That is the system working as designed: your contact is
now in a tally that someone reads.

## Doing better than a call

In rough order of weight:

1. **An in-district meeting** with a staffer. Offices hold constituent hours
   and they are frequently undersubscribed. Go with two or three people and one
   ask.
2. **A paper letter**, posted. Low volume means high attention.
3. **A phone call.** The reliable baseline.
4. **An email.** Counts, but weighted least.

you, and going with someone who has done it before makes the difference.
