The gap between caring about this and doing something about it is usually not
motivation. It is not knowing what the something is. So here is the list,
ordered by leverage per hour.

## 1. Call or write your representative — 15 minutes

The single highest-leverage action available to an individual, and the one
almost nobody takes. Congressional offices count contacts. A dozen calls on an
issue is a signal; a hundred is a position.

Most offices have heard from approximately **nobody** in Pennsylvania about
frontier AI risk. The bar is on the floor.

[Who to contact and what to say](/act/congress)

## 2. Come to a meeting — 90 minutes, once

You do not have to join anything. Come once, see what it is, decide
afterwards. We plan the month, divide up the congressional outreach, and argue
about tactics.

No technical background is useful here. The people who are best at this are
usually the ones who can talk to a stranger, not the ones who can explain a
transformer.

[Details and sign-up](/join)

## 3. Table or flyer — 2 hours

A folding table near a campus or a farmers' market. You will have more real
conversations in two hours than in two months of posting online, and the
reaction is not what people expect — most passers-by already think AI is moving
too fast and are relieved someone said it out loud.

We have the table, the flyers and the scripts. You bring a willingness to talk
to strangers.

[Where we table](/pennsylvania)

## 4. Talk to people you already know — ongoing

Unglamorous and probably the most effective thing on this list. A conversation
with a friend who trusts you moves them further than any advertisement.

Two things that help: lead with the **people building it say so**, not with
scenarios — the credibility does the work. And do not try to win. Plant the
thought and leave it.

## 5. Write to a local outlet — 1 hour

The *Inquirer*, *Billy Penn*, WHYY, campus papers, neighbourhood newsletters. A
letter to the editor from a named local resident still carries weight with
offices, and a published letter gets forwarded internally in ways a tweet does
not.

## 6. Donate — 2 minutes

We are volunteers and our costs are small — printing, table fees,
occasionally a room. National and international advocacy is where money has
the most effect, so we point donations upstream rather than collecting them
here.

[Donate to PauseAI](https://pauseai.info/donate)

## What not to bother with

Being honest about what wastes your time:

- **Arguing with strangers online.** Satisfying, unmeasurable, occasionally
  counterproductive.
- **Signing petitions and stopping.** A signature is a start, not an action.
- **Waiting until you understand it properly.** You know enough. The people
  building it do not understand it either; that is the point.

## If you have specific skills

Design, video, data, local press contacts, legislative experience,
event space, or you teach at Penn, Drexel or Temple — say so when you
[get in touch](/about). Those are the bottlenecks, not general enthusiasm.
