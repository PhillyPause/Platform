Deliberately short. A hundred links is a way of avoiding a recommendation.

## If you have ten minutes

- **The Statement on AI Risk** — one sentence, signed by the people building
  the technology and hundreds of researchers. The signature list is the
  argument.
  [safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk](https://www.safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk)
- **PauseAI's own introduction** — the case in more detail than we give it
  here. [pauseai.info/learn](https://pauseai.info/learn)

## If you have an hour

- **Geoffrey Hinton's interviews** after leaving Google. A Nobel laureate
  explaining, in plain language, why he changed his mind about his own field.
- **Robert Miles' videos** on AI alignment. The clearest explanation of *why
  this is technically hard* rather than merely worrying, for a general
  audience.
- **PauseAI's counterarguments page** —
  [pauseai.info/counterarguments](https://pauseai.info/counterarguments). We
  have [our own version](/ask/objections), shorter and ruder.

## Read the other side

A position you cannot argue against is not a position you hold. These are the
serious critiques, not strawmen:

- **The "AI is normal technology" argument** — that frontier models are a
  continuation of ordinary automation and the existential framing is a
  category error.
- **Regulatory capture critiques** — that safety regulation written now will
  entrench the current labs. We think this is right about the risk and wrong
  about the conclusion; see [objections](/ask/objections).
- **Capability sceptics** — that scaling is hitting limits and current
  approaches will plateau well short of anything dangerous.

If one of these convinces you, that is a good outcome. We would rather be
wrong than be unquestioned.
