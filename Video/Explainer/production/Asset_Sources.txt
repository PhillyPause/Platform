HISTORICAL IMAGERY AND MATERIALS

1. Benjamin Franklin, engraved by H. B. Hall, 1868, after Joseph-Siffred
   Duplessis's 1783 portrait. Library of Congress.
   https://www.loc.gov/item/2004671903/
   File: assets/historical/franklin_hall_1868.jpg
   Rights record: “No known restrictions on publication.” Historical artwork
   published in 1868; public domain in the United States.
   Use: manually masked, sepia-treated Franklin head for the animated puppet.
   A separate engraved torso is also retained as an optional editable asset;
   the film uses an original articulated body and limbs.

2. An east prospect of the city of Philadelphia, [1768], engraved by Thomas
   Jefferys after George Heap, under Nicholas Scull. Library of Congress.
   https://www.loc.gov/item/2004672470/
   File: assets/historical/philadelphia_1768.jpg
   Rights record: “No known restrictions on publication.” Published in 1768;
   public domain in the United States.
   Use: waterfront skyline and the frontal State House inset, separately
   masked and treated in sepia for the distant set and architectural layers.

3. A N.W. view of the state house in Philadelphia taken 1778, [1787], James
   Trenchard after Charles Willson Peale. Library of Congress.
   https://www.loc.gov/item/2004671521/
   File: assets/historical/state_house_1787.jpg
   Rights record: “No known restrictions on publication.” Published in 1787;
   public domain in the United States.
   Use: masked engraved clouds and a crop of the unprinted paper margin.
   The paper is repeated, resized and color-treated across original props.

4. Racknitz — The Turk, plate 3, Joseph Friedrich von Racknitz, 1789.
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Racknitz_-_The_Turk_3.jpg
   File: assets/historical/racknitz_1789.jpg
   Rights record: Wikimedia Commons PD-Art / PD-old-100; faithful reproduction
   of a public-domain engraving. US copyright expired.
   Use: cropped mechanical engraving texture. A circular mechanism cutout is
   retained as an optional asset. The AI puppet's body, face, limbs and gears
   are original geometry; the historical Turk character is not reproduced.

5. Textile, 18th century, Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum,
   accession 1955-45-1-a/c; maker unknown.
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Textile,_18th_century_(CH_18404637).jpg
   File: assets/historical/linen_18th_century.jpg
   Rights record: Commons PD-old-100-expired / Public Domain Mark; the source
   log also records Smithsonian's media listing as CC0.
   Use: cropped, desaturated and tinted textile surface on clothes, curtains,
   scenery and props. This is a museum digitization, not a new physical scan
   of thrift-store fabric made for this production.

6. Optional alternate, unused in the rendered film: Benjamin Franklin L.L.D.,
   envoy from the American Congress to the French Court, [1780], unknown
   engraver, published by R. Faulder. Library of Congress.
   https://www.loc.gov/item/2003675417/
   File: assets/historical/franklin_1780.jpg
   Rights record: no known restrictions on publication in the United States;
   the catalog notes that other countries' laws can differ. Published in 1780;
   retained as a public-domain alternate portrait donor.

ORIGINAL VISUAL ASSETS

art.py and film.py define the original puppet geometry, independent limbs,
automaton template, carriages, stage scenery, jars, cabinet, banners, ledger,
clockwork, fire, lightning, rod, lever and petition. These shapes are filled
with the sourced material textures above and rendered as separate PNG assets.
Cutout masks preserve the historical scans; no generative image replacement
was used. Repeated crowd plates reuse the one original automaton design.

USER BRANDING AND NARRATION

PhillyPause logo: the user's existing website artwork, downloaded from
https://phillypause.org/img/logo.svg
Source: assets/brand/logo.svg. The film's soot-colored rendering preserves
the supplied vector paths. This is user branding, not a public-domain asset.

Narration: the user-supplied PhillyPauseSiteHeroVideo.mp3, retained in the
package as audio/source_narration.mp3. The supplied words and performance
are used; no replacement voice was generated. The recording is not claimed
as public domain or licensed for unrelated reuse.

ORIGINAL MUSIC AND FOLEY

The Paper Lightning Rod: original composition and procedural synthesis in
audio_score.py. Separate stems: audio/music.wav and audio/foley.wav.
Music-box, pizzicato, clarinet-like and accordion-like timbres are synthesized
oscillators and decay models, not recordings of acoustic instruments.
Paper, gears, wood, quill and restrained thunder are synthesized noise/tone
effects. No downloaded samples, soundfonts, third-party melody or film score
were used. See audio/score_metadata.json for the note/effect event list and
measurements. The narration is not included in either instrumental stem.
