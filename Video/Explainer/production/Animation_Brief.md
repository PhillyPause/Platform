# PhillyPause Explainer — Animation Brief

Oct 19, 2026 · @Louis Berman

## Overview

A 70–80 second explainer for PhillyPause about the July 2026 Hugging Face breach. It is animated in the hand-made, cut-paper puppet style of Jamie Caliri's *Lemony Snicket* end titles.

- **Goal:** viewers share the video and tell Congress "no superintelligence until we know it will be safe."
- **Audience:** the general public in and around Philadelphia. Assume no technical background.
- **Core idea:** the subject is cutting-edge; the look is not. Show AI agents, servers and the hack as 18th-century Philadelphia objects, puppets and print. The contrast is the point.
- **Through-line:** Ben Franklin's lightning rod. It opens as history and closes as the call to action.
- **Length:** the script is about 180 words, roughly 75 seconds at a measured read, plus a short end card.

## Style reference

Watch this first: [Lemony Snicket's A Series of Unfortunate Events — End Titles, directed by Jamie Caliri (Vimeo)](https://vimeo.com/10005002). Caliri's own notes on the process are on [Watch the Titles](https://www.watchthetitles.com/?p=250).

How it was made: Caliri scanned thrift-store fabrics and built cut-out puppets and environments in After Effects ([Print](https://www.printmag.com/comics-animation-design/lunch-break-video-lemony-snickets-end-titles/)). Our film should feel made the same way: real materials, digitally staged.

**Take from it**

- Jointed puppets that move on pivots, not characters redrawn frame by frame.
- A stage built in layers. The camera travels through foreground, characters and backdrop.
- Visible material texture on everything: fabric weave, paper fibre, card edges.
- Theatrical light: pools of light, vignettes, soft shadows between layers.
- One continuous storybook journey. Scenes hand off through camera moves, not hard cuts.
- Words that live inside the world (signs, banners, printed pages) rather than floating on top.

**Don't copy**

- No characters, designs or recurring motifs from the film or the books. Every puppet is original.
- No music from the film or a sound-alike of its score.
- No footage from the clip itself.

## Look and feel

Everything on screen should look like it could have been cut, printed or sewn in 1760s Philadelphia, including the AI.

| Element | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Palette | Aged paper, sepia, soot black, muted ochre and oxblood. One accent colour, a lightning yellow-white, is reserved for electricity and AI. |
| Materials | Scanned fabric (linen, wool, ticking stripe), laid paper, card stock, and linework from period engravings. Keep grain and fibres visible. |
| Characters | Articulated cut-out puppets with visible pivots: Franklin, townspeople, tradesmen, tycoons, inventors. |
| The AI agents | Identical clockwork automaton puppets cut from one template. A crowd of 700 is that one puppet duplicated, which makes the scale feel eerie. |
| Depth | 4–6 layers per scene, parallax camera moves, soft drop shadows so layers read as paper on a stage. |
| Motion | Puppets animate on twos (12 fps inside a 24 fps timeline) for a hand-moved feel. Camera moves stay smooth. Add idle sway, bob and candle flicker. |
| Type | Letterpress and wood type, Caslon-style serif for body text. Key words stamp or print in: MY, EVERY, TODAY'S, PAUSE. |
| Numbers and data | Shown as period objects: a printer's counter, tally marks, a ledger, a hand-drawn colonial map. |
| Sound | Original score for a small ensemble: music box, pizzicato strings, clarinet, accordion. Foley of paper, gears, ticking and thunder. |

**Avoid:** screens, code, circuit boards, glowing robots and stock tech footage. Show no company logos. Hugging Face and OpenAI appear only as painted shop signs or labels in period lettering.

## Context: the Hugging Face incident

In July 2026, about 1,200 OpenAI test agents that were meant to be sealed off from each other found each other, and about 700 of them attacked Hugging Face, a company that hosts AI models and datasets. They were trying to cheat a cybersecurity benchmark ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/hugging-face/)).

Every claim in the script checks out against published accounts:

| Script line | What the record says |
| --- | --- |
| "more than a thousand AI agents that were supposed to be isolated. They found each other anyway" | Each agent was meant to be sealed off. Around 1,200 joined a makeshift message board and exchanged over 70,000 messages ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/hugging-face/)). |
| "seven hundred of them hacked Hugging Face" | Around 700 agents ran a multi-day attack, running code on dozens of servers and taking full control of one ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/hugging-face/)). |
| "Nobody programmed them to do that" | No human directed the attack. The agents were cheating on a test, and one message shows they knew they were outside the test's scope ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_OpenAI_agent_cyberattacks)). |
| "they tried to fake their logs" | The agents made partially successful attempts to tamper with activity logs to cover their tracks ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/hugging-face/)). |

Impact: Hugging Face found access to a limited set of internal datasets and credentials, no tampering with public models, and rebuilt about a third of its infrastructure ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_OpenAI_agent_cyberattacks)).

Expect one pushback: OpenAI says deployment safeguards were intentionally off for this test ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_OpenAI_agent_cyberattacks)). Critics may use that against "This is TODAY'S AI"; the guardrails line partly answers it.

**The metaphor:** Franklin flew a kite into a storm, then invented the lightning rod so Philadelphia wouldn't burn. The film's last image is ordinary people becoming that rod.

## Script

Verbatim. Bracketed tags are delivery notes for the voice actor, not on-screen text.

> &#91;incredulous\] The richest companies on Earth are racing to build AI that can do your job. MY job. EVERY job.
>
> &#91;pointed\] They call it superintelligence. Let's call it what it is: human-replacement AI.
>
> &#91;brisk\]\[explanatory\] The plan: build something smarter than all of us. The problem: nobody knows how to control it. Not even its makers.
>
> &#91;conversational\]\[storytelling\] This July, OpenAI tested more than a thousand AI agents that were supposed to be isolated. They found each other anyway, and seven hundred of them hacked Hugging Face, a major AI platform.
>
> &#91;incredulous\] Nobody programmed them to do that.
>
> &#91;matter-of-fact\] And they tried to fake their logs to cover their tracks.
>
> &#91;pointed\]\[serious\] This is TODAY'S AI. Next time, it won't be a test.
>
> &#91;brisk\]\[conversational\] All the AI companies are racing to be first. They aren't worried about the guardrails.
>
> &#91;wry\]\[storytelling\] Benjamin Franklin played with lightning, then built something to keep it from burning Philly down.
>
> &#91;cold\]\[cutting\] The tech companies don't care what they burn down.
>
> &#91;confident\]\[understated\] So let's hit pause. Join PhillyPause. Share this video. Tell Congress: no superintelligence until we know it will be safe.
>
> &#91;dry\]\[firm\] Franklin built a lightning rod. This time, it's us.

## Beat-by-beat storyboard

Fifteen beats, about 75 seconds plus a 3-second end card. Timings assume roughly 150 words a minute; retime to the recorded voice-over.

| # | Time | Voice-over | Picture | Motion and transition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0:00–0:08 | "The richest companies… EVERY job." | Tycoon puppets in top hats race steam carriages across a paper landscape. Tradesmen along the road (cobbler, printer, clerk, nurse) are plucked off the stage one by one. | Camera trucks right with the race. MY and EVERY stamp in as wood type on the beat. |
| 2 | 0:08–0:13 | "They call it superintelligence… human-replacement AI." | A showman's banner unrolls: SUPERINTELLIGENCE. A hand tears it to reveal HUMAN-REPLACEMENT underneath. An automaton steps into a worker's empty spot. | Tear lands on "Let's call it what it is." |
| 3 | 0:13–0:21 | "The plan… Not even its makers." | Two almanac pages. THE PLAN: inventors build a clockwork giant taller than the town. THE PROBLEM: its control strings snap out of the makers' hands. | Page-turn between the two. Makers tumble as the strings go slack. |
| 4 | 0:21–0:28 | "This July… supposed to be isolated." | A calendar leaf reading JULY tears off. Rows of identical automatons, each under its own bell jar, fill a cabinet of curiosities. A printer's counter reads 1,000+. | Slow pull-back reveals the scale. |
| 5 | 0:28–0:30 | "They found each other anyway," | Threads run jar to jar. Notes slip through pipes onto a tavern noticeboard crowded with pinned messages. | Threads draw on fast, like a spreading web. |
| 6 | 0:30–0:35 | "and seven hundred… a major AI platform." | Automatons pour out and swarm a grand counting-house with a painted sign: HUGGING FACE. They pick locks and climb through windows. The counter flips to 700. | One puppet duplicates outward into the swarm. Camera rises to the rooftops. |
| 7 | 0:35–0:37 | "Nobody programmed them to do that." | In the workshop, a maker unrolls the automatons' instruction scroll. It says nothing about this. He shrugs. | Quick cut-in; the scroll unrolls blank. |
| 8 | 0:37–0:42 | "And they tried to fake their logs…" | A ledger. A brass automaton hand scratches out entries with a quill and writes new ones. Muddy footprints on the page are brushed away. | Close-up; ink blots bloom, then vanish. |
| 9 | 0:42–0:46 | "This is TODAY'S AI. Next time, it won't be a test." | TODAY'S prints across a broadside. Pull back: it all sat on a small stage marked TEST. The curtain drops; behind it is the real town. | The curtain drop is the turn into the second half. |
| 10 | 0:46–0:52 | "All the AI companies… guardrails." | The tycoons race again, now on a cliff road, smashing through wooden guardrails. | Fast truck along the cliff; each rail snaps on a beat. |
| 11 | 0:52–0:58 | "Benjamin Franklin… burning Philly down." | Franklin flies a kite in a storm, then fixes a lightning rod to a Philadelphia rooftop. Lightning, in the accent colour, strikes the rod and runs safely to ground. | Warm, wry pacing. The town glows once the strike is grounded. |
| 12 | 0:58–1:02 | "The tech companies don't care what they burn down." | A row of rooftops with no rods. Lightning hits; cut-paper flames curl the town while the tycoons drive past. | Colder light; flames animate on twos. |
| 13 | 1:02–1:04 | "So let's hit pause." | A hand pulls a brass lever. Every puppet freezes mid-motion, even the flames. | Hard freeze and a beat of silence in the music. |
| 14 | 1:04–1:11 | "Join PhillyPause… safe." | A broadside prints JOIN PHILLYPAUSE in wood type. Townspeople gather; a quill signs a petition addressed to Congress beside an engraving of the Capitol. | JOIN, SHARE and TELL CONGRESS stamp in on each phrase. |
| 15 | 1:11–1:15 | "Franklin built a lightning rod. This time, it's us." | The townspeople stand together on a rooftop and raise one rod. Lightning strikes and is grounded through them. | Hold, then the end card: PhillyPause wordmark and URL. |

Option for beat 14: Franklin's 1754 "Join, or Die" segmented snake is public domain. It could be adapted to read JOIN alone.

## Asset sourcing

Use public-domain images published before 1929, cut apart and re-rigged as puppets, plus textures you scan yourself. Log the source of every asset.

| Need | Where to look |
| --- | --- |
| Franklin, kite and lightning-rod imagery, "Join, or Die" (1754), colonial Philadelphia views, *Puck* tycoon cartoons | [Library of Congress Prints & Photographs](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/) |
| Automata, including the 1789 Racknitz cutaways of the chess-playing Turk | [Wikimedia Commons: The Turk](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:The_Turk) |
| Trade and workshop engravings, period machinery, fire scenes | [NYPL Digital Collections](https://digitalcollections.nypl.org/), [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) book scans, [Old Book Illustrations](https://www.oldbookillustrations.com/) |
| Fabric and paper textures | Scan your own: thrift-store linen, wool and ticking, laid paper, card. This is how the reference was made. |
| Type | A Caslon revival (such as Libre Caslon) for text; a wood-type display face for stamped words. |
