Where things stand on November 21, 2026. Two dates decide most of it: Pennsylvania's legislative session ends on **November 30**, and this Congress on **January 3**. A bill that has not passed by then dies, and starts again next year under a new number. If something here has moved, [tell us](mailto:support@phillypause.org?subject=AI%20bills%20page).

## In Washington

**Before Congress goes home.** Two bills have to pass, and anything else that moves this year is likely to ride on one of them: the defence bill, and the government's funding, which runs out on December 11.

- The House's defence bill (H.R. 8800, passed July 22) sets up a Pentagon programme to prepare for artificial general intelligence and requires AI incident reporting. The Senate's draft adds human control over the use of force and an AI biosecurity framework. The final text is being negotiated now ([status](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8800/all-actions), [what is in it](https://theaipn.org/aipn-fy27-ndaa-conference-letter/)).
- **Overriding state AI laws** is the fight that keeps coming back. The White House asked Congress to do it in March ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/03/20/white-house-ai-framework-calls-for-preemption-of-state-laws/)), and it could be attached to the children's package below ([lame-duck preview](https://bgrdc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/2026-Lame-Duck-Preview.pdf)). The House's **FRONTIER Act** (H.R. 9925) would set transparency, audit and incident rules for the largest developers, and stop states adding their own ([bill](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9925)).

**Children and chatbots, the nearest to a vote.** On August 5 the Senate Commerce Committee approved the **CHATBOT Act** (S. 4407), on safe design for chatbots children use, with two more children's AI bills and the Kids Online Safety Act ([status](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4407/all-actions)). The **GUARD Act** (S. 3062), on age checks and AI companions, has waited for a Senate vote since May ([status](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/all-actions)).

**The most powerful systems themselves.**

- The **Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act** would prohibit building it and pause advanced AI work until there are rules. Bernie Sanders introduced it in the Senate in September (S. 5493), and Greg Casar in the House (H.R. 10538), where it has 12 co-sponsors ([House bill](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/10538), [text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s5493is/html/BILLS-119s5493is.htm)). The closest thing in Congress to what we are asking for.
- The **AI Kill Switch Act** (H.R. 9917) would require that the most powerful systems can be shut down, and let Homeland Security order it. It has 14 co-sponsors, the latest on November 10, and its sponsor is pressing for a vote before January ([bill](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9917)).
- The **AI Risk Management and Security Act** (S. 5576) would create an AI Safety Board and give it a look at advanced models 45 days before release. Its sponsors tried to pass it without a vote on September 29, and one senator blocked it ([Sen. Warner](https://www.warner.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/on-senate-floor-warner-discusses-urgent-need-to-pass-ai-security-legislation/)).
- The **Artificial Intelligence Risk Evaluation Act** (S. 2938) would have the government test advanced systems for dangerous capabilities before release. It had a hearing on September 30 ([status](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/2938/all-info)).
- The **AI Whistleblower Protection Act** (S. 1792) would protect people inside AI companies who report safety problems. Seven senators signed on in September ([bill](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1792)).

## In Harrisburg

Nothing on AI is scheduled for the session's last days, so every bill below that has not passed both chambers dies on November 30. The ones worth having will need sponsors to bring them back in January.

**The most powerful systems.** Two House bills would do for Pennsylvania what California and New York already require of the largest AI developers: assess catastrophic risks, submit to outside audits, report serious incidents, and protect whistleblowers. They are [Melissa Shusterman](/act/officials/melissa-shusterman)'s **HB 2800** ([bill](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb2800)) and **HB 2829** ([bill](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb2829)). Both were heard on October 19 and neither got a vote ([WITF](https://www.witf.org/2026/10/19/pa-lawmakers-push-for-ai-safety-rules-as-federal-action-lags/)). In the Senate, [Maria Collett](/act/officials/maria-collett) has proposed the same but not yet introduced it ([memo](https://www.palegis.us/senate/co-sponsorship/memo?memoID=48995)).

**Passed one chamber, stuck in the other.**

- **SAFECHAT Act**, on chatbots and children: passed the Senate 49 to 1 in March and has sat in a House committee since ([SB 1090](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/sb1090)).
- **AI companionship apps**: passed the House 133 to 70 on September 28 ([HB 2006](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb2006)).
- **AI in health care and insurance decisions**: passed the House 144 to 59 on September 30 ([HB 1925](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb1925)).
- **Data centres**: five House bills await the Senate, among them a pause for townships weighing proposals, passed 201 to 1 ([HB 2496](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb2496)), and ending data centres' sales-tax break, passed 197 to 5 ([HB 2198](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb2198)).

**Done this year.**

- **Deceptive AI advertising** must be labelled, under Act 99, signed October 22 and in force from December 21 ([SB 806](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/sb806)).
- **Data centres** over 10 megawatts must report their energy and water use, under Act 21, signed July 12 ([SB 146](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/sb146)).
- The **Governor's executive order** of August 18 makes data centres pay for the grid they need, bans their non-disclosure agreements and requires local approval before state permits ([press release](https://www.pa.gov/governor/newsroom/2026-press-releases/governor-shapiro-signs-executive-order-on-data-center-developmen)).
- The **House told Congress**, 131 to 72 on September 30, not to stop states making their own AI rules ([HR 425](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hr425)).

## At home

- **Philadelphia.** The Mayor set up a data-centre task force on September 16; its report is due June 30, 2027 ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/data-center-task-force-philadelphia-parker/)). Council asked Harrisburg on September 24 to ban or tightly limit licence-plate-reading cameras ([City & State](https://www.cityandstatepa.com/policy/2026/09/philadelphia-city-council-calls-state-lawmakers-address-automated-license-plate-readers/416218/)), but has not held the hearings on data centres it voted for ([Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/politics/philadelphia/data-center-moratorium-philadelphia-ai-20260914.html)). The city's rules for its own use of AI depend on a governance committee it could not show exists in July ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civics/philadelphia-municipal-ai-rules-missing-committee/)).
- **Montgomery.** The Commissioners adopted principles for the county's own use of AI on August 20, and it now publishes [a register of the AI tools it uses](https://www.montgomerycountypa.gov/your-government/administration/boards-commissions/advisory-council-artificial-intelligence-public-good/ai-public-registry). In Limerick, hearings on a 750-megawatt data centre have run since October ([Spring-Ford Press](https://www.springford.press/article/limerick-data-center-traffic-analysis-draws-scrutiny-at-hearing)).
- **Chester.** East Vincent turned down a data centre at Pennhurst in May; on November 10 a court let neighbours join the fight over the developer's appeal ([MyChesCo](https://www.mychesco.com/?p=686322)). The county's planners and Montgomery's have written [a guide for townships](https://www.chescoplanning.org/UandI/DataCenters/) weighing such proposals.
- **Bucks.** Falls Township is rewriting its data-centre rules, up for public discussion this month, though they will not apply to Amazon's approved campus ([Levittown Now](https://levittownnow.com/2026/11/03/falls-twp-preparing-new-data-center-rules-but-they-will-not-apply-to-amazon-project/)).
- **Delaware.** Trainer's zoning officer has said a 120-megawatt data centre on Ridge Road would be allowed by right, with no special approval ([WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/delaware-county-trainer-potential-data-center/)). Chadds Ford adopted rules for data centres in April ([Chadds Ford Live](https://chaddsfordlive.com/2026/04/chadds-ford-passes-data-center-ordinance/)).
