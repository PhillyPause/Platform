## Who answers

Almost never the official. In a congressional office the phone is answered by a staff assistant or an intern, often in their twenties, who answers dozens of calls a day. In Harrisburg and at City Hall it may be the legislator's chief of staff, or whoever is in the district office that afternoon. They are polite, they are busy, and listening to you is their job.

## What they write down

Your name, whether you are a constituent, what the call was about, and what you asked for. Offices keep a tally of calls by topic and position, and the member sees it, usually weekly. That is why the call is short and the ask is plain: the person on the phone needs to know which box to tick.

On AI, very few constituents have called anyone. A handful of calls on a subject nobody else is calling about stands out in that tally in a way a thousand calls on a hot-button issue do not.

## What to say

Thirty seconds is plenty:

1. **Who you are and where you live.** "Hi, my name is Sam, and I live in Kensington, 19134."
2. **One ask.** "I am calling to ask the Senator to back an international pause on building ever more powerful AI systems until they can be shown to be safe."
3. **One reason, if you have one.** "I have two kids and nobody can tell me who is checking these systems."
4. **Ask where they stand.** "Could you tell me where the Senator stands on this?"

The [call page](/act) for each official has the script written out, and can put your own name and reason in it.

## If they ask you something

- **"What is your address?"** They are checking that you are a constituent. Your zip code is usually enough.
- **"Is there a bill?"** "Not yet. I want to know where the Senator stands." That is a complete answer.
- **A question you cannot answer.** "I don't know, but I would like a reply in writing." Then give your email address.

Nobody will quiz you, and nobody expects you to be an expert.

## Voicemail

Leave one. Offices go through their voicemail and log those calls the same way. Say the same four things, slowly, and spell your name.

## When to call

Weekday office hours are best. If the Washington line is busy, the district office takes the message just as well and passes it on.

## Then tell us

There is an "I called" button on the call page. We keep only that the call happened, never what you said, so we know which offices are hearing from people and which are not.
