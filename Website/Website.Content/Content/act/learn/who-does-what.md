title: Who does what on AI
description: What Congress, the President, Harrisburg, City Hall and the counties can each actually do about AI, so you know what to ask whom.
updated: 2026-11-20
---

Everyone who represents you can do something. They cannot all do the same thing, so the ask changes with the office.

## In Washington

**Congress** can pass the laws that would matter most: rules for the companies building the most powerful systems, money for safety research, limits on what can be exported, and support for an international agreement to pause the race. It also decides whether states keep the power to make their own AI rules. Proposals to stop states from regulating AI have come up repeatedly, and so far have been beaten back.

**The Senate** has a particular role in international agreements: a treaty needs two-thirds of the Senate to ratify it. Pennsylvania's two senators each have a vote on that.

**The President** negotiates with other countries and directs federal agencies, and can act by executive order. We do not list the White House here because it is not Philadelphia's to call, but your members of Congress are the ones who answer for what Washington does.

## In Harrisburg

**The General Assembly**, 50 senators and 203 representatives, can pass Pennsylvania's own AI laws. States have already done so: California and New York have both passed laws requiring the largest AI developers to publish safety plans and report serious incidents. Pennsylvania has so far legislated on deepfakes and AI-generated abuse material, not on the systems themselves.

**The Governor** signs or vetoes those bills, sets the state's own use of AI, and leads on where the state wants data centres built.

**The Attorney General** enforces consumer-protection law, which already covers some of what AI products do to people.

## At home

**Philadelphia's Mayor and City Council**, 17 members, ten for districts and seven at large, can hold hearings, pass resolutions that put the city on the record, and set the rules for the city's own use of AI.

**The counties**: Bucks, Chester and Montgomery are run by three-member boards of commissioners, and Delaware County by a five-member council. They can pass resolutions and set their own use of AI. Where data centres go is mostly decided by townships and boroughs, with county planning commissions advising.

A resolution from a city or county does not change federal law. It does tell your members of Congress that their own voters, and the officials who know those voters best, are paying attention.

## So, who first?

Congress. That is where a pause would be decided. Then Harrisburg, then home. The [action centre](/act) shows you all of them for your address, in that order.
